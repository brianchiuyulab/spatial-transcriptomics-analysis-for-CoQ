# Results and interpretation

## Dataset and quality control

The young and geriatric raw matrices contained 77,642 and 78,514 beads, respectively. The deposited final datasets contained 32,104 young and 22,920 geriatric beads after the authors' transcript-count and spatial-neighbor filters. Median raw counts per retained bead were 53 and 47. *Coq8a* was detected in 465 young beads (1.45%) and 243 geriatric beads (1.06%).

## Cell-type and fusion-associated evidence

Within the injury zone, 44 of 149 Coq8a-positive young beads were labeled Fusing Myocytes, compared with an 18.14% Fusing Myocytes baseline among all young injury beads (relative enrichment 1.63; Fisher p = 5.75 × 10⁻⁴; BH-adjusted p = 0.00862). In the geriatric section, 21 of 63 Coq8a-positive beads were labeled Fusing Myocytes, compared with a 14.31% baseline (relative enrichment 2.33; Fisher p = 1.65 × 10⁻⁴; BH-adjusted p = 0.00247).

Fusing Myocytes-labelled regions also showed higher detection of *Myh3*, *Myh8*, *Myog*, *Mymk*, *Mymx*, and *Ncam1* than other injury-zone beads, consistent with the differentiation/fusion-associated profile used by the source study.

## Spatial neighborhood evidence

In the young section, *Coq8a*-positive density around Fusing Myocytes-labelled regions exceeded random injury-zone regions at all tested radii from 25 to 150 µm after correction. In the geriatric section, enrichment was detected at 75–150 µm. This independent-neighbor analysis excludes the focal bead itself and supports localization of *Coq8a* signal around fusion-associated regions.

The injury zone as a whole was not Coq8a-high: detection was 1.30% inside versus 1.53% outside in the young section, and 0.65% versus 1.36% in the geriatric section. Thus the signal is localized to particular injury-zone niches rather than reflecting uniform induction throughout injured tissue.

## Manuscript-ready conclusion

***Coq8a* expression is spatially associated with transcriptionally defined differentiating/fusing myogenic niches during muscle regeneration, supporting *Coq8a* as an exploratory candidate for follow-up studies of muscle differentiation and fusion.**
